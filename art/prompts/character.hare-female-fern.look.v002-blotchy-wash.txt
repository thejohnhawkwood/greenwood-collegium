SEMANTIC ID: hare-blotchy-wash
FAILURE TO CORRECT: hare style-test v001. The hare's coat and fur were one even colour inside a line, like a fill tool. The mouse style master is blotchy watercolour. This pass must match that stain. It is not a pass if the coat is one green.

STYLE MASTER
The mouse reference controls only the way colour is laid down. Copy the tunic's separate moss, olive, and yellow-green puddles, the paper gaps between them, and the pale frayed edge. Do not copy the mouse's face, hood, toggles, or rope belt.

IDENTITY
The hare reference is the character. Tall female hare, long upright ears, brown fur, cream chest, small pale tail, knee-length green coat with buttons down the front, no hood, profile facing left, walking, one forepaw back. Keep that pose and those clothes.

COLOUR, THIS IS THE TASK
Paint the coat as about eight separate watercolour puddles, not one fill. Some puddles are sap green, some dull olive, some yellow-green. Leave irregular white paper between several puddles, especially on the sleeve and the lower coat. The puddles have soft wet edges and do not reach the ink in a straight line. Fur is the same way: the cheek is a pale tan stain, the back is a darker umber stain, the legs are a third browner stain, and those stains do not join into one smooth brown. Do not hatch a flat colour to fake texture. Do not bucket-fill any region.

INK AND EDGE
Keep near-black hooked ink and a pale frayed fringe on the hem and cuffs, as on the mouse. The fringe is not a substitute for blotchy colour inside the coat.

OUTPUT
Isolated figure. Flat background exactly #EE3173. No ground, no signature, no words, no second animal.
